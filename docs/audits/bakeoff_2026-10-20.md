# Generator bake-off (STATUS item 3)

Date: 2026-10-20. Signer: lead. Branch `claude/bakeoff`. Design 5.2; bar from design section 9:
fits the 12 GB card, commercial-use licence, at most 3 minutes per model, and passes the
five-view inspection on at least 3 of the 5 prompts.

## Set-up

- PC: RTX 4070 SUPER 12 GB, one GPU job at a time (`models/gpu.lock`), Windows 11.
- Same five references for every candidate (`tools/bakeoff/prompts.toml`): SDXL 1.0 base drew
  them, BiRefNet (pinned) matted them, once (`make_refs.py`). Only the 3D model varies.
- The brute reference is **two-armed by the director's choice** (2026-10-20, option A): SDXL
  could not draw six arms in 24 seeds over two prompts, so the six-arm test of the generators
  was not possible from a drawn reference. Six-arm monsters will need a hand-made or edited
  reference, or limbs added in the skeleton step.
- Each candidate runs in its own env behind one adapter contract (`tools/bakeoff/adapters/`,
  D-012). Sheets: headless Blender 5.2 Workbench, six orthographic views (front, three-quarter,
  left, back, right, top), clay and, where the model has colour, colour (`render_views.py`,
  `sheets.py`). Raw generator output, no clean-up beyond what each adapter states below.
- Out of the bake-off: Hunyuan 3D (D-010, territory ban), SF3D/SPAR3D (D-011, gated weights),
  Direct3D-S2 (D-017: 20 GB and about 14 min a model as measured, over the bar), TRELLIS
  (non-commercial dependencies).

## Measurements

Times are generation per model after loading; peak is the whole-GPU peak (nvidia-smi) over the
run, with torch's own peak in brackets. Triangles are the raw mesh.

| | Candidate | Licence | Gen per model | Load | Peak VRAM | Raw triangles | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Hi3DGen (Stable-X) | MIT | 12-14 s | 26 s | 6.8 GB (5.2) | 220k-592k | none |
| B | Step1X-3D geometry 1.3B | Apache-2.0 | 16-18 s | 6 s | 5.8 GB (4.8) | 200k (decimated) | none |
| C | TripoSG | MIT | 29-33 s | 25 s | 6.3 GB (5.3) | 674k-1.38M | none |
| D | TripoSR | MIT | 1.1-1.5 s | 10 s | 5.4 GB (4.1) | 42k-116k | vertex colour |

All four fit 12 GB with more than 5 GB to spare and are far inside 3 minutes a model.

Adapter notes (what we changed from upstream, all inside our adapter):
- **Step1X-3D:** its hierarchical decoder leaves NaN vertices at the band edge; the adapter
  drops faces with a non-finite vertex, removes floaters (small loose parts) and decimates to
  200k triangles (trimesh + fast-simplification, MIT). Texture stage not used (27-29 GB).
- **Hi3DGen:** low-VRAM staging, same quality settings: StableNormal runs first and is freed,
  then Hi3DGen's sub-models go to the GPU one stage at a time. Torch peak 13.15 GB -> 5.22 GB,
  generation 14 s (D-017 gave it this one try).
- **TripoSG:** the banned `diso` (CC BY-NC) is stubbed out; scikit-image marching cubes instead.
  Its RMBG-1.4 matting (non-commercial) is not used; we pass our BiRefNet alpha.
- **TripoSR:** torchmcubes (MPL-2.0) marching cubes at 256; vertex colours, no bake.

## Five-view inspection

One fresh `realism-critic` per prompt board (no earlier context, told to find reasons to reject,
2x crops from the 512 px renders), then the lead's own detail check (`art-qa-critic`). Raw
meshes before clean-up: small speckle floaters are noted, not failed.

| Prompt | A Hi3DGen | B Step1X-3D | C TripoSG | D TripoSR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Humanoid (knight) | PASS: clean armour, real back | FAIL: hand-sized floating clumps (melted dagger), flap on the back | PASS: best detail; 298 speckles | PASS on shape, lumpy surface, helmet reads as a hood |
| Spider | PASS: 8 legs, body squashed flat | FAIL: two leg tips detached and floating | PASS: 8 legs, closest stance; speckle "hair" | FAIL: flat relief, back repeats the front |
| Brute (two-armed ref) | FAIL: tube arching over the head, balloon hump, melted face | PASS (just): shorts missing at the back | PASS: closest to the ref, shorts all round | FAIL: lumpy, mitten hands, melted face |
| Fox | PASS: crisp facets, four clean legs | PASS: chin ledge, softer surface | FAIL: hind paw detached, neck twisted 90 degrees | FAIL: melted ears, slab body |
| Bird (robin) | PASS: symmetric; four claw tips float | FAIL: tail is a rod with a spoon tip | PASS: best feet; far wing lifts off as a slab | FAIL: tail split in three, toeless blob feet |
| **Passes** | **4 of 5** | 2 of 5 | **4 of 5** | 1 of 5 |

Every candidate's surface is raw and over budget (42k to 1.38M triangles against the 8k / 2k
export budget), so Step 2 (rebuild, decimate, merge loose bits) runs on whichever is picked.
TripoSR's "weird patterns" (director, on its sheets) are its real surface: a lumpy, stepped
skin from its coarse density field, not a render artefact; the same renderer shows A-C smooth.

## Colour

Only TripoSR outputs colour (coarse vertex colour). Hi3DGen, Step1X-3D and TripoSG are
geometry only, so any of them on the menu needs a colour route before Step 2's bake: project
the reference image onto the mesh (front and back views drawn by the image model), or a
licence-clean texturing model found later. This is Phase 2 work, not part of the pick.

## Result

**Pass the bar (fits 12 GB, commercial licence, under 3 min, five-view pass on at least 3 of 5):
Hi3DGen (4/5) and TripoSG (4/5).** Step1X-3D (2/5) and TripoSR (1/5) do not.

- Hi3DGen: fastest of the two (13 s), lighter meshes, cleanest surfaces; failed the brute.
  Critics ranked it first on humanoid, fox and bird.
- TripoSG: the most detail and closest to the reference (critics ranked it first on spider and
  brute), but 29-33 s and the heaviest meshes (up to 1.38M triangles) with many speckles.
- They fail different prompts (Hi3DGen the brute, TripoSG the fox), which suits a menu (D-018):
  when one misses, the other is one click away.

Fixes made during the review (sheets only, not the meshes' shape): every side, back and top
view was camera-rolled (fixed in `render_views.py`); the glTF importer's quaternion rotation
mode ignored the facing turns (fixed); the TripoSR adapter skipped upstream's export
orientation step (fixed in the adapter; the five saved meshes were turned with the same step,
originals kept). Each generator puts the reference on a different camera, so `sheets.py` takes
per-candidate and per-prompt turns so that "front" always shows the face.

Sheets and boards: `docs/audits/bakeoff/` (per prompt `board_<prompt>.jpg`, per candidate
`<candidate>_<prompt>.jpg`); raw numbers in `docs/audits/bakeoff/summary.json`.
